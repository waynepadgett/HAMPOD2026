Yaesu Reader for FT-857/897 and eventually FT-817
Revised 01/27/2020
Firmware: v1.00b5

OVERVIEW
  The HamPod series of Amateur Radio station accessories are stand-alone, micro-processor based text-to-speech devices designed to provide speech output and accessibility to common station equipment such as transceivers, Radios, antenna controllers and rotor controllers, SWR/Power meters and other equipment capable of serial data exchange. The voice synthesizer utilizes the popular "DoubleTalk" voice synthesizer and is customizable to your personal preferences.

  The HAMPod Yaesu Reader has been specifically designed to work with several Yaesu transceivers to provide as much capability and accessibility to the radio as possible. With the Yaesu Reader you will have access to many parameters of your radio and therefore be able to use your equipment with a high degree of capability. All without the need for a connected station computer or help from a fellow ham or long-suffering partner!

  There are several options that determine how the HamPod Yaesu Reader performs and interacts with your Radio and all options are stored in EEPROM and retain their settings when powered off. The HamPod Yaesu reader can be set to automatically report some changes as you operate your Radio or can be configured to be silent and only report information when requested by you with a key press on the HamPod Yaesu Readers 16 button keypad. The keypad is used to configure the HamPod Yaesu Reader and to request information from your Radio.


GENERAL DESCRIPTION
  The rear of the HamPod has two male DB9 serial ports and a DC power jack. The serial ports are identified as serial port 1 and serial port 2. The connector located next to the power jack is port 1. With the HamPod Yaesu Reader, you can connect two supported pieces of equipment and switch between them by using the keypad as described later in this manual. Serial port 1 is also used to connect to a computer serial port using a null modem adapter or cable to flash the firmware in the HamPod Yaesu Reader when an upgrade is available. See Appendix C for upgrade instructions. The power jack accepts 9 to 15 volts dc and has a positive center pin. New versions of the HamPod also have a 3.5mm headphone jack on the right side. There is also a small hole at the lower left corner of the speaker grill which provides access to a reset button inside the enclosure. This hole is located within the very bottom groove of the speaker grill and about one quarter of an inch from the left end of the groove. You can use a paper clip to reach into the box to push this button. The button is about one inch below the top cover. Be careful not to poke through the speaker cone if you happen to be somewhere above the very bottom  groove of the speaker grill area. Cycling power to the HamPod will essentially do the same thing and may be easier. This button will reboot the HamPod Yaesu Reader without changing any configuration settings. This does not reset or effect your connected device.
Important firmware Update note:
As of 02/01/2019, the HamPod is now shipping with a new version of the Microchip CPU processor which has twice the flash memory available for firmware. This is needed to accommodate some combinations of firmware builds that require the extra memory space. When downloading firmware from the HamPod.com web site, you need to be sure to get the version for the processor chip currently in your HamPod. Firmware versions that starts with v1.xx is for the older CPU while v2.xx if for the new CPU. Check the manual for your HamPod to see how to access the version number. On most radio firmware, you can do this by attempting to send a frequency of 777 to the radio.


CONNECTING THE HamPod Yaesu READER
IMPORTANT!
A.  It is a good idea to not apply power to the HamPod until you have connected it to your Radio. This is because the serial port jacks on the rear of the HamPod are male connectors and it is possible to short one of these pins to the grounded shell while trying to engage the female end of the serial cable into this connector.
B.  Currently, all Yaesu transceivers supported by this firmware have a female 8 pin MiniDin serial data port connector on the rear of the radio. So you need to use a special male 8 pin MiniDin to female DE-9 DSub serial cable to connect to the HamPod. These cables contain some active level shifting circuitry as part of the cable so be sure to get a cable that has the DE-9 DSub connector on one end and not a USB connector. 
1. Connect one end of the female end of the supplied serial cable to the RS-232 serial port on the rear of the HamPod using the port configured for Yaesu Radios as determined by the firmware build you received or loaded.
2. Connect the other end of the cable to the 8 pin MiniDin serial connector on the rear of your Yaesu Radio.
3. Connect the supplied 9 volt DC, 800MA wall power unit to the HamPod . This is a center pin positive 2.1mm x 5.5mm coaxial power connector. You may also connect this using your own cable to your shack 13.8 volt DC power distribution system if desired. There is no on/off switch as the HamPod draws minimal current (about 20mA), when not in use.

  The Yaesu Reader communicates with an Yaesu Radio at a baud rate of 4800, 9600, or 38,400 depending on the HamPod configuration baudrate setting and the radio port setting. If possible, you should set both the HamPod and radio to 38,400 baud for best performance. When powered up, the Yaesu Reader will play a quick Morse letter "R" and announce "Ham Pod" and then will look for a connected Yaesu Radio. When found, the Yaesu Reader will report "Yaesu", letting you know it has established a connection with your Radio. The Yaesu Reader then continuously polls for the Radio at the configured baud rate at about once a second to monitor the connection.


KEYPAD
  The layout  of the first 12 keys of the 16 button keypad is similar to a standard 12 key telephone keypad with an additional column of 4 keys to the right. The 16 push buttons keys are arranged as 4 rows of 4 columns and each key is capable of two different functions depending on the current mode of the HamPod. They are operated with both a standard push or an extended longer push and hold action. With the addition of a "SHIFT" key, each key can generate up to four unique operations. A second shift level called "Shift 2", will then provide an additional 2 unique key actions. A synopsis of the switches and their functions are listed in Appendix A for quick reference. 
		As previously mentioned, the first three columns of keys are identically labeled as a standard 12 key telephone keypad and  are similar in layout to most radio's that are equipped with a numeric keypad. The fourth column of keys are primarily configuration and modifier keys.

  In this document, the key labels are enclosed within square brackets. For reference, left to right, top to bottom, the keys are labeled:

Row 1: [1], [2], [3], [A]
Row 2: [4], [5], [6], [B]
Row 3: [7[, [8], [9], [C]
Row 4: [*], [0], [#], [D]

  The keys are always pushed individually. Never is more than one key pressed simultaneously to perform any function.

  There are five primary modes of operation for the keypad when used with an Yaesu FT-857, 897 and 817 Radio and is as follows:
1. Normal or key Mode. Standard user query and control operations.
2. Frequency Entry Mode. Convenient frequency entry for your Yaesu.
3. SETMode. Convenient entry of some data and control levels on supported Yaesu models.
4. DTMF Mode. Use the HamPod keypad to generate touch tone audio. 
5. Configuration Mode. This allows setup of the HamPod voice parameters as well as Yaesu Reader options.

2. Frequency Entry Mode:
  Pressing the [#] key will prepare the HamPod Yaesu Reader to accept a frequency from the keypad.  After hearing the VFO prompt, enter your desired frequency
inserting a decimal point (star key) where needed and press the [ENTER] key to send it to your radio. Frequency entry format is the same as used when entering
a frequency directly on the radio itself. 

A few special notes here: 
1. A second press of the [DECIMAL POINT] key while entering a frequency will clear your entry and return the HamPod Yaesu Reader to the normal mode. 
2. Once you initiate frequency entry, the [D] key also functions as a clear key. 
3. If the Keypad Timeout option is enabled, the HamPod Yaesu Reader will clear the partial entry and return to normal mode it you exceed the timeout duration.

4. There are two special frequencies you can enter which will perform the following functions: 
A.  enter a frequency of 777 and press the [ENTER] key. This will announce the product information and the firmware version number currently installed.

B.  enter a frequency of 999 and press the [ENTER] key. This will restore the HamPod EEPROM to the factory default settings and perform a reset. You can
do this if the settings ever get scrambled or if you just want to restore to the default settings.

5. CONFIGURATION MODE
  This mode allows changes to the various voice settings as well as modifying the options which effect how the  HamPod operates and interacts with your Yaesu transceiver. To enter Configuration Mode, press and hold the [C] key for 1 second. When you enter the configuration menu, you are placed at the option you last viewed when you previously exited the configuration menu.

In Configuration Mode the keypad switches perform the following functions:
[A]: Step forward through the configuration options.
[B]: Step backward through the configuration options.
[C]: Increment the parameter values.
[D]: Decrements the parameter values.
These are circular menus and will cycle through once  the first or last choice has been passed.

To exit and save your changes, press and hole the [C] key for 1 second. The settings are stored in EEPROM and will remain even when power is removed.

To exit without saving your changes, press and hold the [D] key for 1 second. Any changes you may have made will not be stored in EEPROM but those changes will still remain in effect until you power off or reset the HamPod. If the key timeout function is active and you don't press a key before the timeout occurs, you will exit configuration mode without saving to EEPROM.


HamPod Yaesu READER CONFIGURATION OPTIONS
1. Key beep: enable or disable. This sounds a beep when a key is pressed.
2. Key Timeout Duration: disable / 5 - 30 Seconds:  When disabled, no key time out will occur. When set to 5 - 30, in both Frequency and Configuration Modes, this sets a time out  period when a key is pressed which will return the HamPod to Normal Mode if another key is not pressed before the set time out period expires.
3. Verbosity: on/disable Some keys and controls on the transceiver generate serial data when manipulated by the user and if this option is enabled, the HamPod Yaesu Reader will automatically announce that data as you operate the radio. This might be useful at times but can get pretty chatty. This option also adds some additional speech labels to various readings. Note that automatic Announcement of VFO frequency when changing frequency by use of the VFO encoder controls are not controlled by this option. 
4. Frequency Plus Mode: on/disable When enabled and you perform a keypad query for the VFO frequency of choice, an additional letter abbreviation will be appended to the read out depicting the current operating mode for that VFO. Some examples are:
5. Communications Timeout: 5-20 This is an internal HamPod value that dictates how long the Yaesu Reader will wait for a response from the transceiver before generating a time out error message. Smaller values allows slightly better performance but can result in more time outs occurring than if larger values are selected. Choose the smallest number that works reliably with your setup
6. Yaesu Baud Rate: Choices are 4800, 9600, and 38,400.
7. DTMF Tone Duration:  (200Milliseconds) The range is from 100 to 500 Milliseconds. This sets how long the tone will sound when pressing a key while in DTMF mode.
8. Power On To Port:  (default is port 1) Selects which serial port is active when the HamPod is powered on.
9. Reset or Flash: Used to reset the HamPod or prepare for flashing. When at this option, you can press either the [C] or [D] keys to alternate between enabling or disabling the opportunity to reset or flash. When the HamPod informs you to "Enter to Reset", you can then press the [Enter] key to initiate the reset. If you do choose to reset, the HamPod will perform a 3 second countdown before performing the reset process. This is useful when you want to coordinate the reset with the application running on your PC for the purpose of updating the firmware. This reset only occurs to the HamPod and not the transceiver. You are also given the option to press the [*] key to cause the HamPod to set itself up for flashing. See appendix C for additional flashing instructions.

HamPod  DoubleTalk VOICE PARAMETERS
  There are nine parameters which effect the voice synthesizer output.
Volume: 0 to 9. Default is 5.
Speed: 0 to 13. Default is 5.
Pitch: 0 to 99. Default is 50.
Articulation: 0 to 9. Default is 3.
Expression: 0 to 9. Default is 3.
Reverb: 0 to 9. Default is 0.
Formant Frequency: 0 to 99. Default is 50.
Tone: 0 to 2. Default is 1.
Voice: 0 to 10. Default is 0.

The last parameter in the above list is the "Voice" parameter and are predefined voice configurations. Please note that many of these predefined "Voices" will override some of the voice parameters such as pitch, tone, and reverb settings that you may have already modified and will be changed when you select one of these alternate "Voices".

FIRMWARE UPGRADE OVERVIEW
The procedure to flash the firmware is standard practice as with other devices that utilize a bootloader. There are basically two components used for this procedure:
1.  The bootloader which is already resident within your HamPod.
2.  A Windows based computer running an application that communicates with the bootloader and sends the new firmware to the HamPod.

The bootloader is a special application that resides within the HamPod and runs every time the device is either reset or  powered on. This special application looks for a unique command sequence over serial port 1 of the HamPod which is connected to your computer which is running the companion Windows flash loader  application. Once the bootloader begins, you have approximately 1.5 seconds to initiate the flash loader application running on the computer. During that first 1.5 second window of opportunity,the bootloader is in control of the HamPod and is looking for a connection from the computer over the serial port. When a valid connection is established between the HamPod and the application running on the computer, the flash loader application then sends the new firmware to the HamPod. If no connection can be established, the bootloader times out and the HamPod starts running as usual.

  An improved method to flash the HamPod has been added to the configuration menu. This greatly simplifies the required timing synchronization  between the HamPod and the flashing application running on the computer.
All previous methods for flashing the HamPod still exist but this new method is much easier to perform since it automatically restarts the HamPod for you.

The following steps will walk you through the flashing process:
1.  Important! You must use a null modem serial cable for this connection. Connect a null modem serial cable between serial port 1 of the HamPod (the connector nearest the DC Power jack), and a serial comm port on your personal computer. The comm port on your computer can be any serial port from COM1: to COM8:. The existing straight wired serial cable provided with your HamPod can be used provided you also use the included null modem adapter in addition to this cable.
 2.  Download and extract the ZIP archive update  firmware file to any directory of your choosing on your computer. You may over write any existing files with a new firmware update if you have previously performed this update before. This file can be found from the appropriate page on the HamPod web site at the following URL:
http://www.HAMPod.com

The files contained within the ZIP archive are:
ds30LoaderConsole.exe : The command line flash loader application.
Flash_1.bat : Batch file when using comm port 1 on PC.
Flash_2.bat : Batch file when using comm port 2 on PC.
Flash_3.bat : Batch file when using comm port 3 on PC.
Flash_4.bat : Batch file when using comm port 4 on PC.
Flash_5.bat : Batch file when using comm port 5 on PC.
Flash_6.bat : Batch file when using comm port 6 on PC.
Flash_7.bat : Batch file when using comm port 7 on PC.
Flash_8.bat : Batch file when using comm port 8 on PC.
***.hex : the firmware file to flash to the HamPod (this file name varies depending on your connected devices)
Other informational text files

3.  Once you extract the files into a directory of your choosing, simply run the appropriate batch file for the comm port number on your PC that you have connected to the HamPod. The batch file contains the command to launch the flash loader  application and also set some necessary parameters for the program. When you start the batch file, the file will open a DOS window, validate the new hex firmware file, and prepare to flash the HamPod but will stop and wait for any key to be pressed on the PC before actually initiating the flashing process. The text written into this DOS window by the flashing application should be readable by most screen readers such as JAWS or Window EYES and will inform you of its progress.

4.  Once the application on the computer pauses and is waiting for a key press, enter the configuration menu on the HamPod with a long press of the [C] key. Scroll through the menu items using the [A] or [B] keys until you reach the "Reset and Flashing Disabled" menu item. Press either the [C] or [D] key to enable this item and you will hear, "Enter to Reset or Star to Flash Firmware". Press the [*] key and the HamPod will enter flash mode with the prompt, "Waiting for Firmware, Any Key to Abort". The HamPod is now waiting and watching for the application on the computer to begin sending the new firmware. At this point, if you press any key on the HamPod, it will exit flash mode and reset itself. To initiate the new firmware transfer from the computer, press any key such as the Space Bar on the computer and it will begin sending the firmware to the HamPod. When you pressed the Space Bar key on the PC, the flash loader application tries to establish a connection with the bootloader within the HamPod and if found, the HamPod will respond with a "Flashing" announcement. This will take about 30 to 45 seconds to complete. DO NOT power off the HamPod while the update is in progress!When finish, the HamPod may restart on its own but sometimes you may need to power cycle the HamPod yourself. The flash loader application on the PC will write a series of period characters to the screen as it transfers the firmware file to the HamPod, When finished, it will report the status of the update and wait for a final key press on the PC to terminate the flash application and close the DOS window.

Note for users of older firmware:
  If you are using an older version of firmware, this new flashing method will not be available and you must use one of the previous methods of synchronizing the HamPod with the PC by performing an manual reset of the HamPod.  The easiest way to do this that usually works is to start the flash loader application on the computer and press any key when prompted to initiate the firmware download. You then have about 20 seconds to reset the HamPod while the PC application continues to look for the bootloader to start running on the HamPod. So at this point, you need to quickly reset the HamPod by use of the reset button, reset configuration menu item, or removing and applying power to the HamPod. In some cases an erroneous character will be sent to the PC by the HamPod during the reset which might confuse the application running on the computer and it will fail. If this happens, just try the procedure again or use one of the alternative methods to reset the HamPod.

5.  After a successful update, you can verify the new firmware has been installed by requesting the product information from the HamPod as specified in the user manual for your specific connected device.
6.   Disconnect from the PC and reconnect the HamPod to your equipment and enjoy the new benefits and features of the firmware!

MENU OPERATION 
The following steps are performed on the radio and describe how a menu item is examined or changed.
1. Press and hold in the [F] key for one second. The Menu Item number and a title for the Menu Mode will appear in the display. 
2. Rotate the MEM/VFO CH knob to select the Menu Item you wish to work on. 
3. When you have chosen the desired Menu Item number, rotate the DIAL knob to change the value or condition for the Menu Item. 
4. When you have made your selection, press and hold in the [F] key for one second to save the new setting and exit to normal operation.

    The FT-857/897 and FT-817, all store their settings in EEPROM along with other radio specific configuration data. If this data is accidentally corrupted, it may result in a "bricked" or damaged radio. Therefore, I chose to not write directly to the EEPROM and use the radio itself to modify these settings. 
  Tapping the "F" key (just to the right of the three soft keys) under the display will bring up the screen menus and holding the "F" key for about one second will get you into the 91  menu configuration options. 
The most notable thing about these menus is the fact that the radio will beep when it is at 001 in the configuration menu and also at the first screen menu so you can always work from that known point. That is crucial as you can then follow along on the menu list below by counting clicks of the encoder. 
There is a HamPod query ([0], hold), that will return the current menu configuration number so you can validate where you are in case you loose count! 
The issue here is that you need to exit the menu system on the radio in order for the radio to update the EEPROM so it will be a valid number. That is also the case with most settings within the configuration menu. 
You will need to exit after making a change and saving it, then query with the HamPod to verify the new setting. Unfortunately, there is no way to access the screen text directly.
